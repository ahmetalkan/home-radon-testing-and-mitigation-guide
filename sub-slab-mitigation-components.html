Sub-Slab Radon Mitigation Components and Inspection Points

Active soil depressurization (ASD) is the standard mitigation architecture for homes with concrete slabs: a small continuous-duty fan draws soil gas from beneath the slab through a sealed PVC pipe and exhausts it safely above the roofline before radon enters living air.

Key items to check on a mitigation proposal

  • Fan placement outside conditioned living space: To avoid pushing pressurized radon gas through any tiny pipe leak into bedrooms, the fan belongs in an unconditioned attic, attached garage attic, or outdoors—never inside a basement living area.
  • U-tube manometer (vacuum gauge): A liquid-filled visual gauge mounted on the vertical suction pipe lets you see at a glance whether the fan is still pulling suction; unequal liquid columns indicate active draw.
  • Sealed sump pit cover: Open sump crocks short-circuit suction and leak soil gas; an airtight gasketed lid with viewing ports allows pump maintenance while preserving negative pressure.
  • Roofline discharge clearance: Exhaust vents above the roof eaves and well clear of windows, dormers, and fresh-air intakes.
