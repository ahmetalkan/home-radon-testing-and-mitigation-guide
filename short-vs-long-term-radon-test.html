Short-Term vs Long-Term Radon Testing: Placement and Timing

Indoor radon fluctuates daily and seasonally with barometric pressure, soil moisture, and heating-system stack effect. Choosing the right test window depends on whether you are screening during a home purchase or tracking your year-round living average.

Test typeTypical durationPractical use
Short-term passive kit (charcoal / electret)2 to 7 daysInitial screening or real-estate transaction window under closed-house conditions
Long-term passive kit (alpha-track)90 days to 1 yearReflects seasonal swings across heating and cooling months
Continuous radon monitor (CRM)48 hours to ongoingShows hourly trends and flags sudden pressure-driven spikes

Closed-house rules for short-term tests

For tests shorter than four days, EPA protocols specify keeping exterior windows and doors closed (except normal entry and exit) starting 12 hours before and throughout the test, and keeping whole-house fans off. Place the detector at least 20 inches off the floor and away from high humidity. Additional accuracy notes appear in continuous radon monitor accuracy on RadonLab.
